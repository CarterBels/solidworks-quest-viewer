Your SolidWorks model, life-size, in your hands.

Send a part or assembly from SolidWorks to a Meta Quest with one click. It appears at 1:1 scale in your room: walk around it, pull it apart, cut through it, and move parts the way their mates allow.

Built on Onshape Quest Viewer by almostinventor (MIT License). The Quest app is a modified copy of that project's viewer; the SolidWorks add-in takes the place of its Onshape Chrome extension.

Features

Everything runs on your local network. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.

One-click send

A Quest Viewer tab in SolidWorks. Click Send to Quest, or say "Marvin, send this to the Quest". Colours and assembled positions come across; send again after an edit and it replaces the old one where you left it.

True 1:1 scale

Appears at real size in mixed reality. FIXED (on by default) keeps it at exactly 100%; turn it off to scale with two hands.

Big assemblies

Repeated parts are sent once, and heavy meshes are simplified to fit the headset. A 1,700-part robot went from 19 M to 1 M triangles in seconds.

Move by mates

MOVE: pinch a part to turn or slide it as its SolidWorks mates allow. Linkages follow step by step and stop at limit mates. FIX PART holds parts still.

Explode and isolate

Clap, or press EXPLODE, to pull the parts apart. With ISOLATE, parts you pull out stay where you leave them, or settle onto a table.

Section cut

CUT gives you a frame you can pinch, move and tilt; everything on one side is cut away, with coloured cut faces.

Part specs

INSPECT: touch a part to see its name, file, configuration, material, size, volume, area, mass and how many are in the model.

Marvin, voice control

Say "Marvin" and a command: "explode", "rotate", "reset", "tablet"… Offline speech recognition on the headset. A glow at the edges of your view shows what he heard.

Hand gestures

Draw a flat circle with your finger to spin the model, clap to explode, double-pinch to reset its scale, pinch the air with both hands to scale it in place.

Built-in help

CONTROLS on the keypad: touch any button to see what it does, what to say and the gesture for it, plus a list of every gesture.

Install

Two pieces: the app on the Quest, and the add-in in SolidWorks. About 15 minutes the first time.

What you need

  • A Meta Quest 3 or 3S with hand tracking on.
  • SolidWorks 2025 on Windows (other recent versions should work too).
  • The PC and the Quest on the same Wi-Fi / network.
  • A USB-C cable, once, to put the app on the Quest.

Put the app on the Quest

  1. Turn on developer mode: in the Meta Horizon phone app, open your headset's settings → Developer mode → on. (The first time, Meta asks you to create a free developer organisation at developer.oculus.com.) Restart the headset.
  2. Download SolidWorksQuestViewer.apk.
  3. Install it, either way:
    • With SideQuest (easiest): install SideQuest on the PC, plug in the Quest, put the headset on and allow USB debugging, then drag the APK onto SideQuest.
    • With adb: install Android platform-tools, plug in the Quest, allow USB debugging in the headset, then: 
      adb install -r SolidWorksQuestViewer.apk
  4. On the Quest, open Library → (dropdown at the top) Unknown Sources → SolidWorks Quest Viewer. Allow the microphone if you want voice commands.

Add the add-in to SolidWorks

  1. Download SolidWorksQuestSender.zip and unzip it.
  2. Close SolidWorks.
  3. Right-click install.ps1 → Run with PowerShell, or in PowerShell in that folder: 
    powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File install.ps1
    Click Yes when Windows asks for administrator rights (SolidWorks reads its add-in list from there).
  4. Start SolidWorks and open a part or assembly: a Quest Viewer tab appears. If it doesn't: Tools → Add-ins → tick Quest Viewer.

Windows may warn that the script was downloaded from the internet: choose to run it once. To remove the add-in later: install.ps1 -Uninstall.

Pair them

  1. Start the app on the Quest. Press the gear on the keypad: the CONNECTION screen shows an address (like 192.168.x.x:8723) and a 6-character code.
  2. In SolidWorks: Quest Viewer → Quest settings, enter both, Save, then Test connection.
  3. Open a model and click Send to Quest. It appears in front of you.

The Quest's address comes from your router and can change; the CONNECTION screen always shows the current one. Reserving an address for the Quest in your router keeps it fixed.

How to use

Every action has a keypad button, and most also have a voice command and a gesture. In the headset, press CONTROLS and touch any button to see this for it.

The keypad floats low on your left. Pinch its ribbed side grips to move it; say "Marvin, tablet" to bring it back. Buttons marked appears when… only show once what they depend on is on.

Help

"Test connection" fails
  • Check the address and code on the Quest's CONNECTION screen (gear page) and enter them again; the address can change when the Quest reconnects to Wi-Fi.
  • The PC and the Quest must be on the same network (guest networks often block devices from seeing each other).
  • Allow SolidWorks through the Windows firewall on private networks if Windows asks.
The Quest Viewer tab doesn't appear in SolidWorks

Tools → Add-ins → tick Quest Viewer (and "Start Up" to keep it on). If it isn't listed, run install.ps1 again with SolidWorks closed and accept the administrator prompt.

A part won't move in MOVE

It may be held: held parts glow orange. Pinch it again to free it, or double-tap FIX PART to free everything. MOVE uses the top-level mates only (a sub-assembly moves as one piece); gear, cam and belt mates aren't followed.

Marvin doesn't hear me

Allow the microphone for the app (Quest settings → Apps → Permissions). Speak "Marvin" clearly, wait for the blue glow, then the command. A noisy room makes it harder.

Where are the settings and the log?

%APPDATA%\SolidWorksQuestSender\settings.json (address, code, triangle budget) and log.txt in the same folder. The Q icon in the Windows notification area shows the status.
